# Income plan for the tools (saved 17 Oct 2026)

Treat the tools as a way to bring in visitors, not as the product you sell. Earn from what those visitors need next. While traffic is low, start with income that pays the most per visitor, or that comes with its own buyers. Leave ads for last.

## Start now

### 1. Agency leads (highest value per visitor)
Most tool users are small businesses: salons on GiftStub, freelancers on InvoiceKit, sellers on LabelFit. One web-design client is worth more than years of ad income.
- Add a soft, relevant line to the business tools at the moment the user downloads their file. For example: "PDF ready ✓ · Need a website for your salon? Rooh Sites builds them."
- Make sure roohsites.com is live with a clear offer, prices or "from" prices, and a contact form. Every tool's footer credit links there.

### 2. Templates sold on marketplaces (pays soonest)
Etsy, Teachers Pay Teachers (TPT) and Gumroad already have buyers, so we don't need Google rankings first. Each product can come from engines we've already built:
- **On TPT:** ABA data-sheet bundles (GoalTally), A/B day posters (BellBook) and homeschool transcript kits (GradLedger).
- **On Etsy:** salon and spa gift certificate packs (GiftStub) and raffle ticket templates (TicketRun).

Each listing also links back to the free tool, so it brings in visitors too.

### 3. Affiliate links
These are plain links with no tracking scripts, so they don't break the privacy promise.
- **Products:**
  - thermal printers and label rolls (LabelFit, PrepLabel)
  - ticket and card stock (TicketRun, GiftStub)
  - label makers (PanelDex)
  - microphones and webcams (StreamWeek)
- **Software:** paid tools to recommend once users outgrow ours, like accounting software (InvoiceKit) or shipping software (LabelFit). Many software affiliate programs pay a monthly commission for as long as the customer stays. Check each program's actual terms before choosing.

### 4. Email list
A simple "Get new free templates" signup turns one-time visitors into people we can reach again for template launches, affiliate offers and new tools.

## Later (months 6–12, once traffic is real)
- **Optional paid upgrades** on the 1–2 tools people come back to (InvoiceKit, PrepLabel, GoalTally). The core stays free; a one-time payment removes the credit and adds batch processing and saved presets. The site kit already has the switch for this (`allFree` in config.js).
- **Direct sponsors per niche,** like a label-printer brand on LabelFit. A simple sponsor card, with no tracking.
- **Ads,** only on guide pages and only once a site passes about 20,000 page views a month. Never inside the tool itself. Needs a consent banner and an updated privacy page.

## Groundwork first
- **Hosting:** every income source above counts as commercial use, and Vercel's free (Hobby) plan is limited to non-commercial personal use. There are two options:
  - Vercel Pro, about $20 a month.
  - Move to Cloudflare Pages, which is free and where the DNS already lives. This is a one-time migration, and it includes switching to Cloudflare's cookie-free analytics.
- **Payments:** use Lemon Squeezy or Paddle (about 5% + 50¢ per sale). They act as merchant of record and handle global VAT/GST.
- **Keep growing traffic:**
  - directory listings for backlinks (`marketing/directory-listings.md`)
  - daily Pinterest posting (`marketing/cowork-pinterest-prompt.md`)
  - one new tool at a time, only in niches with proven demand

## Order and timing
1. Hosting decision.
2. Agency lines on the business tools.
3. The first 2–3 marketplace products.
4. Affiliate links.
5. Email list.

Agency leads and marketplace sales can start within weeks. Affiliate and email income builds over 3–6 months. Paid upgrades, sponsors and ads come after 6–12 months.

Related: `marketing/paid-tool-research.md`. That research found that cheaper paid copies of big global tools mostly fail, because free tools already do the core job.
