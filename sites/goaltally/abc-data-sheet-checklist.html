Quick answer: An ABC data sheet checklist is the tick-box version of ABC recording. For each incident you tick what happened right before (the antecedent) and right after (the consequence) from a short list, write the behavior in the middle column, and tick a possible function. It takes seconds instead of a paragraph, and the ticks are easy to count across many incidents.

What the ABC checklist looks like

The lists are a starting point. The antecedent options are demand or instruction given, told “no”, a transition, attention given to others, a preferred item removed, alone or unstructured time, and noise or crowding. The consequence options are verbal redirection, reprimand, demand removed or delayed, item or activity given, attention from an adult or peer, planned ignoring and a break.

How to count an ABC checklist

  1. Collect the sheets from every setting and staff member into one pile, in date order.
  2. Tally the ticks: count how often each antecedent, each consequence and each possible function was ticked.
  3. Look for what repeats. For example, 7 of 10 incidents followed a demand and ended with the demand removed.
  4. Take the tally to the team. A pattern is a hypothesis to test, not a diagnosis. The behavior team decides what to do next.

To see how often incidents happen from day to day, tally them on a frequency data sheet, or graph the weekly counts in the ABA graph maker.

Checklist or narrative ABC?

Use the checklist when you already know the usual antecedents and consequences and want quick, countable data. Use the narrative sheet when you are still learning what typically happens: its open columns catch details a list can't. Many teams start narrative, then build the checklist from those notes. Both are on the ABC data sheet page.

Make the checklist fit your student

  • Keep each list to 6 to 8 options and keep the “Other” line, so staff never have to force a tick.
  • Use the words your team uses (“transition to lunch”, not “transition”) so everyone ticks the same thing.
  • Write the behavior and its definition at the top of the sheet, so every observer records the same thing.
  • Print one student's sheets only for that student, and use initials, not full names, on anything that travels.

Frequently asked questions

What is an ABC checklist?

The tick-box version of an ABC data sheet. Instead of writing a sentence for the antecedent and the consequence, you tick them from short lists, with an “Other” line. The behavior is still written in your own words.

Is there an ABC data sheet checklist I can edit?

Yes. In the generator on this page, edit the antecedent and consequence lists, one option per line, and the sheet prints them as tick boxes. You can also download the blank PDF and write the options in by hand. Nothing you type is uploaded.

How do I tally an ABC checklist?

Count the ticks in each column across all the sheets: which antecedents, which consequences and which possible functions come up most often. Treat the biggest counts as a hypothesis for the team to check.

Does the checklist tell me why the behavior happens?

No. It shows what tends to come before and after the behavior. The function is a guess that a team confirms with more data, interviews and, where needed, a functional behavior assessment led by a qualified professional.
