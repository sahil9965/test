ABC Data Sheet

Record what happened right before, during and right after each incident. Choose a quick tick-box checklist or open write-in columns, edit the options, and print.

Quick answer: An ABC data sheet records the Antecedent (what happened just before the behavior), the Behavior (what the student did, described objectively) and the Consequence (what happened right after). Collected over several incidents, ABC data helps a team spot patterns and form a hypothesis about the behavior's function, such as escape, attention, access to items or automatic reinforcement.

Checklist or narrative?

LayoutStrengthsLimits
Checklist (structured)Fast to complete; easy to tally patterns; consistent between staff.Limited to the listed options, so keep an “Other” line.
Narrative (open columns)Captures details you didn't anticipate.Slower to write, harder to summarize, and easier to slip into interpretation.

Many teams start with narrative ABC to learn what typically happens, then build a checklist from those notes. Both layouts are available above, and the antecedent and consequence options are editable.

How to fill in an ABC data sheet

  1. Note when and where: the date, time, setting and activity.
  2. Antecedent: what happened just before. A demand, a transition, being told no, attention given to someone else, a loud noise.
  3. Behavior: what the student did, in words anyone could check (“threw the pencil and shouted ‘no’”), with duration and intensity if relevant.
  4. Consequence: what happened right after: what adults and peers did, and what the student got or avoided.
  5. Possible function: a staff hypothesis, to be confirmed by looking at many incidents together.

Common functions of behavior

  • Escape or avoidance: the behavior ends or delays something unpleasant, such as a task.
  • Attention: the behavior gets a reaction from adults or peers, positive or negative.
  • Tangible: the behavior gets access to an item or activity.
  • Automatic (sensory): the behavior is reinforcing in itself, whatever others do.

Tips

  • Write it up as soon as possible after the incident.
  • Describe what you saw and heard, not what you think the student intended.
  • Collect incidents across different times, activities and staff.
  • ABC data alone doesn't confirm a function. It's one part of a functional behavior assessment led by a qualified professional.

Frequently asked questions

What does ABC stand for?

Antecedent, Behavior, Consequence: what happened before, what the student did, and what happened after.

How many ABC entries do I need?

There's no fixed number. Collect enough incidents across different settings and times to see a pattern; your behavior team will say when there's enough.

Can I change the antecedent and consequence options?

Yes. In checklist layout, edit the option lists, one per line. They print as tick boxes with an “Other” line.

Is an ABC chart the same as an FBA?

No. ABC recording is one descriptive part of a functional behavior assessment (FBA), which also includes interviews, other observations and sometimes a functional analysis.
