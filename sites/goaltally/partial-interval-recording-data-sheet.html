Partial Interval Recording Data Sheet

Divide the observation into equal intervals and mark each one in which the behavior happened at all. Choose the interval length and the sheet lays out the grid with times.

Runs entirely in your browser. Use student initials, not names.

Quick answer: In partial interval recording you split an observation into equal intervals, commonly 10 or 15 seconds, and mark an interval if the behavior happens at any time during it, even once and briefly. The result is the percentage of intervals scored: scored intervals ÷ total intervals × 100. It's an estimate rather than a count, and it tends to overestimate how much of the time the behavior actually happens.

How to do partial interval recording

  1. Choose the interval and observation length. 10-second intervals for 10 minutes gives 60 intervals. The sheet prints each interval's end time so you can follow a stopwatch.
  2. Start a timer and watch the student for the whole interval.
  3. Mark the box if the behavior happens at any point in the interval. One occurrence is enough; don't mark the same interval twice.
  4. Count and divide: marked intervals ÷ total intervals × 100.
  5. Graph % of intervals. On a tablet, the interval timer highlights the current box and beeps at each change.

Choosing the interval length

Shorter intervals give a closer estimate but are harder to score while teaching. Many observations use 5 to 15 second intervals with a timer or audio cue. Keep the interval and the observation length the same each session so the percentages can be compared; the sheet prints both in the key.

Worked example

Off-task behavior was observed for 10 minutes using 10-second intervals, so there were 60 intervals. It occurred during 18 of them: 18 ÷ 60 × 100 = 30% of intervals. That means the behavior happened in 30% of intervals, not that the student was off task for 30% of the time. For the share of time, use duration recording.

Frequently asked questions

Why does partial interval recording overestimate?

An interval is scored even if the behavior lasted one second of it, so a short behavior can make a whole interval count. That's why it suits behaviors you want to reduce: it won't make progress look better than it is.

Can I record more than one behavior?

Yes, up to three. Each box shows letters (A, B, C) to circle, and the summary line gives a percentage for each behavior.

What if I miss an interval?

Cross it out as not observed and leave it out of the total, so 1 missed interval out of 60 means dividing by 59. When scoring on screen, leave the box blank and lower Intervals observed by one for each missed interval.

Is partial interval the same as interval recording?

It's one of three interval-based methods, alongside whole interval recording and momentary time sampling.
