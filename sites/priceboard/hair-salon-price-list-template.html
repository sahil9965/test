How to organise a hair salon menu

Clients look for the service first and the price second, so order sections the way they think about a visit: cuts and styling, then color, then treatments and extensions if you offer them. Put the most-booked service at the top of each section. If you do a lot of special occasion work, a separate “Bridal & events” section with trials and updos makes it easy to find.

Name services the way clients ask for them. “Partial highlights” and “Balayage” are searched and asked for by name; an in-house name like “Sun-kissed package” needs a note underneath saying what it includes. Notes are also the place for what's included (“includes toner and blow-dry”) and how long a result lasts (“lasts up to 4 months”).

Starting prices and add-ons

Write 45+ for a starting price; PriceBoard prints it as $45+. Anything that isn't a plain number, like “from $45”, “$90 / 60 min” or “Free”, prints exactly as you type it. In a color section most prices will be starting prices, and that's normal. What clients dislike is a surprise, so explain in the fine print what moves the price: length, thickness, extra product and extra time. Add-ons like a gloss, a bond-building treatment or extra color bowls can go in their own short section, so the main prices stay simple.

Frequently asked questions

Should a hair salon list starting prices or exact prices?

Most salons list starting prices for color and long-hair services, because the time and product depend on the client's hair. Exact prices work for short, predictable services such as a men's cut, a blow-dry or a toner. If you list starting prices, add a line of fine print saying what makes the price go up, so nobody is surprised at the till.

How do I show different prices for short, medium and long hair?

Either list them as separate services, for example “All-over color, short” and “All-over color, long”, or keep one line and put the range in the price box, such as “$110–$150”, with a note like “by length”. Separate lines are clearer when the gap is large.

Should I list each stylist's level?

If your prices differ by stylist level, add a short section or a note under the services that change, for example “Senior stylist +$15”. Keep it to one rule that the front desk can explain in a sentence.

What fine print do hair salons usually add?

Common lines are: prices are starting prices, a consultation is needed for color corrections, the cancellation or late policy, and whether gratuity is included. Keep it to two short sentences so it is actually read.
