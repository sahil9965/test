Organising a lash and brow menu

Lash clients compare styles first, so list your full sets together, from classic to the fullest volume, then fills and removal. Lifts, tints, lamination and brow shaping make a natural second section. If you also do waxing or facials, add them as a third section rather than mixing them in.

Most lash menus need a few rules in writing: how long a fill is valid, how much of the lash line must remain for a fill, and how to arrive. These fit in the fine print at the bottom, and a QR code can open your booking page with the full policy and aftercare.

Frequently asked questions

How should I list lash fills?

List fills by how long it has been since the last appointment, for example “Fill (within 2 weeks)” and “Fill (within 3 weeks)”, and say in the fine print when a fill becomes a full set. The Lash & brow sample shows one way to word it.

Where do I put my prep rules?

In the fine print: “Please come with clean lashes and no eye makeup” is short enough to print. Longer aftercare instructions belong on your booking page or a separate card.

Can I show prices for each lash artist level?

Yes. Either add a short note such as “Master artist +$20” under the services it applies to, or make a section for each artist level if the menus are different.

What size works best for a lash studio?

A Half Letter or 5 × 7 in menu in a sign holder suits a small room, and the story size suits Instagram, where most lash clients find their artist.
