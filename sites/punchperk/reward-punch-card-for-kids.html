Reward Punch Card for Kids (Free Printable)

For parents and teachers: a punch card for reading nights, chores and new habits, with a prize at the end. Print several at once, 10 per page.

Quick answer: The Reading & chores preset above gives you a 10-punch card with book marks and a filled prize spot in purple. Type the child's reward, change the terms line to the habit you are working on, and print. Each page holds 10 cards, so one sheet covers a family or a small reading group.

What the Reading & chores preset prints

The front shows “My Reading Rewards” in purple, ten open-book outlines in two rows of five and a filled prize spot at the end. The reward line says “Read 9 nights, pick a reward”, and the terms line says that a grown-up punches the card after each finished night or chore. The back thanks the child with “Keep reading, keep growing!”. Change the wording to suit your family, your classroom or a library program.

Make the goal small enough to win

Children respond to goals they can see themselves reaching. A reading card that fills in a month is a long wait for a six-year-old. Choose the number of punches to match how often the habit happens. For a nightly habit, 7 punches is a week; for a habit that happens three times a week, 6 punches is two weeks. The punch count in PunchPerk runs from 5 to 12.

  • One habit per card. Reading, tidying a room, practicing an instrument or brushing teeth. Mixed cards are harder to follow.
  • Punch right away. The sooner the punch follows the habit, the stronger the link.
  • Give the reward on time. A reward that is promised and delayed teaches nothing good.

For parents

Print a few cards on card stock, keep a hole punch or stamp on the fridge and let your child choose which punch mark to use. Letting kids pick the icon, the colour and the reward makes them more likely to care about the card. A star, heart or smiley suits most children, and the paw print is popular with animal fans. Add your child's name on the Name line.

Don't worry about the serial numbers and staff initials boxes; they are designed for businesses and are switched off in this preset.

For teachers and librarians

A reading challenge works well with a punch card. Hand one to every student at the start of the challenge, give a punch for each book finished or each night of reading logged, and award a bookmark or a prize at the end. A class of 25 needs 3 pages at 10 cards per page. Try the behavior punch card for classroom conduct goals.

For a library program, turn on the back and add the library's address and opening hours, with a QR code to the events page or the catalogue. The thank-you line can carry the program name.

Printing and keeping the cards

Print on card stock for cards that survive a school bag or a fridge door. If you only need a few, use the plain-paper layout with crop marks: 8 cards per page, cut with scissors or a trimmer. Pre-cut 3.5 × 2 in business card sheets suit a bigger group. A cheap sleeve or a bit of tape stops the card from tearing, and a ring of cards held together with a binder clip keeps a family set tidy.

Frequently asked questions

How many punches suit younger children?

For ages 4 to 6, try 5 or 6 punches so the reward arrives within a week. Older children can handle 10 or 12 for a month-long goal. A reward that arrives soon matters more than a big one that is far away.

What are good rewards that don't cost much?

Time and choices work well: pick a family movie, stay up 15 minutes late, choose the weekend breakfast, have a friend over or get a trip to the library. Match the reward to the effort, and let the child help choose it.

Should a punch card be used for chores?

Many families use one for new habits and short-term goals. Keep the list small, make the rules clear, and don't punch for basic things the child is expected to do every day. The card is for building a new habit, not for paying for everything.

Can I make a card for each child?

Yes. Print a card for each child, change the Name line, or give siblings different colours so they can tell them apart. The palettes change the accent colour in one click.

Can the card be reused?

A card with a clear reward at the end is easy to reset. Print a spare batch and start a new card when one is finished, or laminate a card and mark it with a dry-erase pen.
