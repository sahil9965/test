Estimate vs quote vs bid: what is the difference?

The words overlap, and customers use them loosely. Here is how common sources describe each one, and how to make yours clear.

Quick answer: An estimate is generally described as an approximate price, a quote as a firmer itemized offer, a bid as an offer in competition (often on larger jobs) and a proposal as a fuller document with scope and terms. Say on the page which one you mean.

How the words are commonly used

Trade and accounting sources describe the four documents in broadly the same way, while noting that the terms overlap and that people use them loosely (FreshBooks, Projul, Let's Build).

DocumentGenerally described asOften used for
EstimateAn approximate price and timeline, which can change as the job is uncovered.Early conversations, jobs with unknowns, repairs.
QuoteA firmer, itemized price for defined work, usually with a time it is valid for.Well-defined jobs where the customer wants a number.
BidAn offer made in competition with others, often on larger jobs or by subcontractors.Commercial work, tenders, subcontracting.
ProposalA fuller document that adds scope, schedule, terms and sometimes plans or options.Larger or more complex jobs.

Why it matters to your customer

A customer comparing two documents needs to know whether each price is a rough guide or a firm offer, and whether both cover the same scope. A one-line note at the top does most of the work: "This is an estimate: the final price may change if the work changes" or "This is a quote: the price holds until the valid-until date."

How to choose

  • Use a quote when the work is clear and you are happy to hold the price for a set time.
  • Use an estimate when something could change the work, and say what.
  • Use a proposal when the job needs more explanation, options or a schedule.
  • Use a bid when you are competing for a larger job against other companies.

Whichever you send, include the basics

Scope of work, labor and material costs, dates, inclusions and exclusions, and payment terms appear in the sources above and in Housecall Pro's template. Our guide to writing a contractor estimate goes through them one by one.

Frequently asked questions

Is a quote legally binding?

Several sources describe a quote as a fixed-price offer that becomes binding once the customer accepts it, but rules vary by place and by wording. Check your local rules, and write the terms down (what the price covers, how long it holds, how changes are handled).

Which should a handyman send?

Whichever matches the job. A fixed price for a well-defined repair suits a quote. If the work is open-ended and the final cost may move, call it an estimate and say what could change it.
